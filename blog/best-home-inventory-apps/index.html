The Best Home Inventory Apps in 2026, Compared

Itemize Team · Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

We make one of these apps, so take that as disclosure, not a disqualifier — the honest answer is that "best" depends heavily on what you're using it for. Here's how the main options actually differ.

What "best" depends on

Before comparing apps, it's worth being clear about what you're optimizing for, because these tools solve genuinely different problems:

  • A personal home inventory for insurance — documenting what you own, room by room, for a claim that might happen someday.
  • A business or asset inventory — tracking stock, equipment, or assets with barcodes, quantities, and check-in/check-out.
  • A one-time claim reconstruction — you're filing a claim right now and need something fast, not a long-term system.

Most of the apps below lean toward one of these three, even when they market themselves as doing all of it.

Itemize

Built specifically for the personal-home-inventory case: your house is organized as floors → rooms → places → items, matching how a house actually exists rather than one flat list. You photograph an item and it can auto-fill the title, category, and estimated value from the photo. Everything syncs to the cloud, and you can export a formatted, room-by-room report when a claim actually happens. The tradeoff is that it's built for homes, not warehouses — there's no barcode scanning or bulk stock management, because that's not the problem it's solving.

Sortly

A strong general-purpose inventory tool with barcode/QR support, custom fields, and multi-location tracking — genuinely good if you're running a small business, tracking equipment across job sites, or managing stock. For a home inventory it works but feels like using a business tool for a personal job: more configuration than most households need.

Encircle

Popular with insurance adjusters and restoration contractors doing on-site documentation professionally — fast photo capture built for claims work at scale. Less aimed at an individual homeowner maintaining their own ongoing inventory over years; it's built for professionals documenting other people's homes, not for you documenting your own.

Nest Egg

A long-standing personal home inventory app with solid manual entry and valuation tracking. Less automated than newer photo-first tools — you're typically typing more and relying on AI-assisted tagging less.

The free NAIC tool

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners offers a free basic home inventory app (myHOME Scr.APP.book). It's a reasonable zero-cost starting point for a simple, one-time pass through your house, but it's basic by design — minimal organization, no cloud sync built for ongoing use, and no automated tagging.

How to actually choose

If you're tracking business assets or equipment across locations, use Sortly. If you're a contractor or adjuster documenting properties professionally, Encircle is built for that workflow. If you just want a free one-time pass and won't maintain it, the NAIC tool is fine.

Worth knowing

If you want an ongoing, photo-first inventory of your own home that's organized the way your house is actually laid out and stays current with minimal effort, that's specifically what Itemize is built for.

The app that wins isn't the one with the most features — it's the one you'll actually keep using after the first week. Whichever you pick, the thing that matters is that it gets used consistently, not which logo is on it.
